    Respirator: GVS Elipse Integra P100 (SPR549)

    Community equipment — shared under Cattail Commons Co-op. Primary use: glass crushing/cullet processing. Also available to borrow for dust work (insulation, clay plaster mixing, sanding).

    Why this mask

    Glass crushing throws fine glass dust and silica particulate into the air — the kind of sub-micron particulate a basic dust mask doesn't stop. This model carries a P100 rating, NIOSH's highest particulate filter class, rated to capture ≥99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns. The "Integra" version adds a built-in goggle, so eye protection and respiratory protection share one seal — useful around a crusher where glass fragments and dust are both a hazard. Size is S/M.

    Specs

    Spec Detail
    Model GVS Elipse Integra P100, SPR549, S/M
    NIOSH approval P100, TC-84A-8119 (meets 42 CFR 84)

    Filter media HESPA (High Efficiency Synthetic Particulate Air), pleated, interchangeable

    Valve Nylon body, silicone diaphragm, large central non-return exhalation valve

    Operating range -5°C to +55°C
    Replacement filter part # SPR321 (twin pack, fits S/M and M/L)

    What it's for

    • Glass dust and cullet particulate from crushing operations
    • Silica dust, cement dust, stone/aggregate dust, wood dust, mold, and similar construction particulates (this is why it doubles for treehouse insulation/plaster/sanding work)
    • Situations needing simultaneous eye protection from flying fragments plus dust protection

    What it's NOT for

    • Gases or vapors of any kind — this is a particulate-only (P100) filter. It will not protect against solvent fumes, paint vapors, ammonia, or other chemical vapors. (GVS sells separate OV/gas-cartridge Integra variants for that — this is not one of them.)
    • Oxygen-deficient atmospheres — not a substitute for supplied-air or SCBA equipment.
    • A compromised or unclear face seal — facial hair along the sealing surface, damaged straps, or a cracked mask body all break the seal and void the protection. Do not use if any of these apply.
    • Continuous/indefinite reuse of a single filter set — see replacement guidance below.
    • Anyone who hasn't checked the fit — S/M is one size; if it doesn't seal well on your face, it isn't providing rated protection regardless of the filter.

    Filter replacement

    Replace filters when breathing becomes noticeably harder (a sign the filter is loading up with particulate) or if you can smell/taste contaminants through the mask (sign of a saturated filter or bad seal). Filtration efficiency stays constant at ~99.97% throughout the filter's usable life — it doesn't gradually leak more particulate, it just gets harder to breathe through until you swap it. Given glass-crushing dust load, expect this to be sooner rather than later — don't wait for a fixed calendar interval.

    Care & maintenance (shared/community equipment)

    Since this is passed between users, hygiene matters more than it would for a personal respirator:

    1. After each use: wipe the mask body (not the filters) with a damp cloth and mild soap and water. Do not submerge the mask or filters in water — filters are water-resistant, not washable.
    2. Let it air dry completely before storing or handing off to the next person.
    3. Before each use: inspect the mask body, straps, and filters for cracks, damage, or wear. Don't use if the strap elastic is shot or the mask body is cracked.
    4. Storage: keep it in a clean, dry place away from direct sunlight and extreme temperatures — ideally sealed in a bag or container so the filters aren't picking up ambient dust between uses.

      Note: Currently in a banjo case on the shelf.

    5. If passing between different users regularly: consider keeping a small stock of spare filters on hand (see above) so a fresh filter can go in when hygiene or fit is a concern, rather than everyone sharing the same filter set indefinitely.
